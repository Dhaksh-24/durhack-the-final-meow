Advanced: map from angled phone photos

      

      For when the tracker needs each phone's pixel-to-floor calibration. Otherwise, draw the room above.
      
Turns a photo from each phone into the room above, in metres. A sheet of paper on the floor tells each photo how big things are. The photos stay in this browser and are never uploaded.

      
    
        
  1. Measure the room: its width along the back wall and its depth.
    2. 
        
  2. Lay a rectangle of known size flat on the floor where a phone can see it, long side parallel to the back wall: a flip-chart sheet or a 1 m square of tape works best, A4 at a pinch. Measure its back-left corner from the left wall and the back wall.
    3. 
        
  3. Add a photo from each phone, taken from where it will stand. Easiest: run python snap.py next to multicam.py (same cameras) and add the files it saves in baseline/, one per camera, named after the camera.
    4. 
        
  4. Click the sheet's four corners. The room's grid appears; press Turn the sheet until it lines up with the walls.
    5. 
        
  5. For each piece of furniture, click the two ends of its front edge where it meets the floor, name it, then build the room. This browser remembers the room (not the photos) until you load another.

        Camera calibration for the tracker
        
For each calibrated phone, the matrix that turns a pixel on the floor into metres. Save it as baseline/calibration.json next to multicam.py and every sighting it logs gets a floor position in metres.

Test it on simulated days

      
Each run plays out a random day, then loses one item at a moment when no phone can see it. Trace's search is compared with a simple rule: check where it was last seen, then its usual places, then your pockets, then outside. These are simulated results: they test the reasoning, not the cameras.

        Load a different room
        
Paste the zone map from the mapping stream and the 3D room is rebuilt from it. Coordinates are in metres and optional; zones without them are laid out automatically. The furniture type (sofa, bed, chair, counter, shelf, hook, desk or table) is worked out from the name if you leave "type" out, and item habits follow the type.
